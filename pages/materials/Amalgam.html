Amalgam

OHD:0000001 · amalgam dental restoration material Metal In progress FDA Class II · 21 CFR 872.3070

A metal dental restoration material that consists of alloy that is formed by reacting mercury with silver, copper, tin and sometimes palladium, zinc and other elements to improve handling characteristics and clinical performance.

Adverse effects and safety

Mercury release and environmental disposal concerns Environmental

Concerns over mercury release in the body and environmental impact after disposal have driven a global phase-down under the Minamata Convention.

1 evidence item
PMID:24683067 · Direct composite resin fillings versus amalgam fillings for permanent or adult posterior teeth. · Supports Systematic review or meta-analysis
“there have been concerns over the use of amalgam restorations (fillings), relating to the mercury release in the body and the environmental impact following its disposal”
States the two concerns directly.

Contact hypersensitivity presenting as a lichenoid lesion of the mucosa in contact with the restoration. Evidence not yet attached.

Dental amalgam is a device that consists of a combination of elemental mercury, supplied as a liquid in bulk, sachet, or predosed capsule form, and amalgam alloy composed primarily of silver, tin, and copper, supplied as a powder in bulk, tablet, or predosed capsule form, for the direct filling of carious lesions or structural defects in teeth.

Permitted uses

  • Direct filling of carious lesions or structural defects in teeth Direct restoration · amalgam filling OHD:0000041

Product codes EJJ (alloy, amalgam), ELY (mercury), and OIV (dental amalgam) all sit under this regulation. FDA issued updated recommendations in 2020 advising certain high-risk groups to avoid amalgam where appropriate; those are not yet captured here.
