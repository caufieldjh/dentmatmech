An endosseous dental implant is a prescription device made of a material such as titanium or titanium alloy that is intended to be surgically placed in the bone of the upper or lower jaw arches to provide support for prosthetic devices, such as artificial teeth, in order to restore a patient's chewing function.

Permitted uses

  • Surgical placement in the bone of the upper or lower jaw to support prosthetic devices Implant · dental implant procedure OHD:0000248 · jaw bone UBERON:0001708

Restrictions

  • Prescription device

This regulation governs the implant as a device, of which titanium is the named material. Product codes are DZE (root-form), NRQ (blade-form), and OAT (orthodontic endosseous implant). Titanium crowns and frameworks fall under other regulations not yet recorded.
