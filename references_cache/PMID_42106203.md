# Prevalence of metal allergy in patients treated with intraoral prostheses and restorations: A systematic review and meta-analysis.
**Authors:** Afroz S, Amina A, Ansar H, Samreen A, Yusufi FNK, Rahman SA
**Journal:** J Prosthet Dent (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.prosdent.2026.04.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.prosdent.2026.04.005)

## Content

STATEMENT OF PROBLEM: Metal alloys used in dentistry are biocompatible with the oral environment; nonetheless they may rarely lead to adverse reactions in susceptible individuals. Differentiating an irritant from an allergic reaction is paramount because the symptoms are nonspecific, and the treatment approach may differ. Therefore, knowing the prevalence of dental metal allergy (DMA) diagnosed by patch test reactivity in individuals reporting symptoms is important.

PURPOSE: The purpose of this systematic review and meta-analysis was to estimate the prevalence of DMA in participants with intraoral metallic restorations or prostheses reporting allergic symptoms confirmed by patch testing.

MATERIAL AND METHODS: English language articles published in Medline/PubMed and Scopus databases reporting allergic symptoms associated with dental metal prostheses or restorations in adults were sorted. Only those studies where epicutaneous patch testing was performed to diagnose the condition in symptomatic participants were included. Two teams of reviewers performed the review separately for clinical studies and case reports. Quality assessment of included studies was done using the Joanna Briggs Institute critical appraisal checklists, with separate tools for observational and case studies.

RESULTS: A total of 27 articles and 31 case reports were found to be eligible for data syntheses. A high heterogeneity between studies (I²=97.1%, τ²=0.0575, Q-test P<.001), a wide 95% prediction interval (3% to 91%), and a lack of clinically relevant data made it difficult to estimate the prevalence of DMA. Specific offending metals were nickel, gold, palladium, cobalt, chromium, elemental mercury, organic mercury, inorganic mercury, dental amalgam, silver, platinum, copper, zinc, tin, and indium. Quality assessment showed that the overall included studies were of medium quality. Clinical manifestation and clinical relevance were not consistently reported.

CONCLUSIONS: Assessing the true prevalence of DMA is difficult because of high heterogeneity across the studies and inconsistent clinical relevance. As the appropriate treatment involves avoiding the allergen by removing the offending metal, it is important to establish clinical relevance before definite treatment is initiated.
